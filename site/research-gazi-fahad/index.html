BA Thesis: Impact of Multiple Intelligences on English Language Classrooms in Tertiary Education

Research Project Duration: January 2021 – September 2021

This qualitative study examines how Gardner’s Multiple Intelligences framework is used in Bangladeshi tertiary English classrooms. Drawing on interviews with faculty and focus groups of students, it finds MI-informed activities (notably visual-spatial) boost engagement, while limited training, materials, classroom space, and AV resources hinder full implementation. It recommends targeted teacher training, modest tech upgrades, and MI-aware materials.

Supervisor: Dr. Shaila Sultana

Research Interest

My research examines how teachers’ knowledge, beliefs, and digital practices interact with classroom design to shape language learning outcomes in resource-constrained higher-education settings. My work blends theory from teacher cognition and digital literacy with pragmatic interventional research. It empirically maps teacher capacity and classroom constraints, design low-cost, scalable pedagogical interventions and materials, and evaluates implementation and learner impact using replicable, mixed-methods designs.

Methods and Evidence Strategy

I favor pragmatic mixed-methods that balance internal rigor with external validity. Typical studies combine instrumented teacher and learner surveys, structured classroom observation protocols, quasi-experimental pre/post designs where randomization is not feasible, and semi-structured interviews for implementation analysis. Analytic tools include basic inferential statistics (SPSS) and clear measurement rubrics so that interventions can be repurposed for multi-site comparative work.

Project: Assessment of Technological Infrastructure, Teacher Competency, Systemic Agility, and Pedagogical Adaptability for Future Pandemic Readiness in HEIs

Funding Agency: Mawlana Bhashani Science and Technology University

Amount: 100,000 BDT

Executive Summary:

The COVID-19 pandemic precipitated an abrupt and largely unplanned shift from face-to-face instruction to online-based teaching and learning across the higher education sector. Institutions worldwide were compelled to move their courses online and adopt what has been termed emergency remote teaching (ERT) , a response that is conceptually and operationally distinct from the preplanned, purpose-built distance-education programs that preceded it. Whereas conventional distance education is designed in advance around known infrastructural and pedagogical parameters, ERT was improvised under conditions of acute uncertainty, and the transition demanded of teachers, students, and administrators not merely psychological endurance, but a combination of instructional resilience, technical competency, and institutional resourcing that many higher education institutions (HEIs) had not previously planned to develop.

A substantial body of research produced in the years following the onset of the pandemic examined how higher-education teachers navigated this transition. However, much of this literature has concentrated on the evolving role of the teacher within the Virtual Learning Environment (VLE) considered in isolation, rather than on how that role interacts with the technological infrastructure available to the institution, the systemic agility of the institution as an organization, and the pedagogical adaptability required at the classroom level. Studies in this vein have tended to describe the pressures placed on teachers to improvise interactive forms of instruction, while offering comparatively little empirical support on the institutional and infrastructural conditions that made such improvisation possible, difficult, or in some cases impossible. Within the context of Bangladesh specifically, competency in navigating a fully virtual teaching environment remains, for a considerable proportion of higher-education faculty, an unfamiliar undertaking, and the institutional response to the pandemic disruption has not yet been systematically documented or evaluated.

It is also worth noting that despite operating in an era of rapid technological change, decision-makers within many HEIs continue to equate technology integration in teaching with comparatively superficial applications, for instance, the use of presentation software with elaborate visual transitions, rather than with the deeper infrastructural, competency-based, and organizational capacities that determine whether an institution can sustain the quality of instruction under disruption. The existing literature, while distinguishing in general terms between the duties, roles, and competencies expected of teachers in virtual settings, has not consistently specified what is meant by "competency" in this context, nor delineated teachers' functions with the precision needed to inform training design. Many of the studies conducted to date have also been restricted to single-institution settings, which limits the extent to which their findings can be generalized for a global setting.

This projects responds to that gap by proposing to test, for the first time within a single empirical design, a four-construct framework of pandemic readiness for higher educational institutions (HEIs), comprising Technological Infrastructure, Teacher Competency, Systemic Agility, and Pedagogical Adaptability. Rather than examining teacher competency or technological infrastructure in isolation, as much of the prior literature has done, the proposed study is designed to examine how these four constructs relate to one another, so that the resulting evidence base can meaningfully inform how HEIs prepare for future disruptions of a health-related, ecological, or socio-political nature.

Project: Integrating Technology in English Language Teaching and Learning at Tertiary Level Education in Bangladesh: Ethical Use and Challenges

Funding Agency: Mawlana Bhashani Science and Technology University

Amount: 100,000 BDT

Executive Summary:

The purpose of this research is to investigate the challenges and possibilities of technology in developing the English language skills of Bangladeshi tertiary-level students. Thus, the research explored whether or not English Language teachers view technology-enhanced language learning and teaching as useful methods as they are popularized. The study investigated the perceptions of teachers and students regarding technology, how teachers integrate technology in the classroom, the obstacles to integrating technology, and teachers’ recommendations for devising an effective technology integration plan in tertiary setting.

The literature review indicates that technology enhances learners' cognitive ability, promotes customized learning, increases interactivity with peers and materials, fosters self-direction among learners, creates a learner-centered atmosphere etc. However, there is a lack of research on the effectiveness of technology in English Language Teaching and Learning in Bangladesh. There is a lack of evidence describing which technological instruments are effective for developing students' specific language skills.

To provide insights and in-depth data for this research, the study observed a qualitative-quantitative methodology using interviews with teachers, and focus group discussions with undergraduate students and, tertiary level classroom observations. The data were analyzed thematically and the results are used to devise a plan for the integration of technology into English Language Teaching and learning in Bangladesh.

The teachers gave recommendations on technology integrated classrooms in tertiary education by removing the challenges, i.e., lack of student and teacher training, lack of awareness, lack of uninterrupted internet supply, lack of necessary digital tools, reluctance to integrate technology etc. They provided four suggestions in order to support the teachers to integrate technology in the English language classroom, which are, teachers training, technological advancement, classroom setting, and material development.

Digital Literacy and Self-Efficacy of English Language Teachers in South Asia

Pedagogical interventions using machine-generated language have advanced sufficiently to investigate the current efficacies and competencies of practitioners responsible for classroom instruction. This study examines the intersection of digital literacy and instructional self-efficacy among English language teachers in South Asia. In an era of expanding educational technology, it addresses how teachers’ technological proficiency relates to their confidence and teaching effectiveness. Employing an integrative literature review and thematic analysis, the research synthesizes findings from diverse studies on technology integration, teacher training, and digital skill development in South Asian classrooms. The analysis reveals a strong positive association between teachers’ digital competencies and their self-efficacy. The review notes significant variation in proficiency: younger or recently trained educators often demonstrate stronger digital fluency, while others may rely mainly on basic skills. Educators with higher proficiency in digital tools,from basic productivity software to advanced content creation and collaboration platforms,report greater confidence in integrating technology into language instruction. This, in turn, leads to more innovative, engaging, and effective teaching practices. Conversely, shortcomings in advanced digital skills, limited access to reliable infrastructure, and inadequate institutional support often undermine teacher confidence and impede the adoption of new pedagogical tools. The study concludes that overcoming these barriers requires structured, ongoing professional development and context-specific integration of technology. It underscores the importance of tailored training programs and supportive institutional policies that align with the actual needs of language teachers. By reinforcing teachers’ digital capacities through targeted resources and sustained support, education systems can empower educators to leverage technology in pedagogically meaningful ways. Ultimately, these measures contribute to closing the digital divide and enhancing language learning outcomes across the region.

Emotional Intelligence and Academic Performance: The Mediating Role of Academic Engagement

This study aims to investigate the impact of emotional intelligence (EI) on academic performance (AP) through the mediation effect of academic engagement (AE). The research was conducted as a pilot study of the researchers’ main project among students in the Bangladeshi higher educational institutions. The pilot data were collected from 62 Master’s level students selected from the Faculty of Business Studies and Faculty of Science of Mawlana Bhashani Science and Technology University. A self-administered questionnaire was used for gathering data, and SPSS 25 and SmartPLS 4.0 software were employed to analyze the data. To test the hypothesized relationships, the study employed Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Model (PLS-SEM) approach. The results revealed that EI does not have a direct effect on AP (β=0.062); however, it exerts an indirect influence through AE (β=0.176), indicating a full mediation effect of AE. The variance (R2 ) of EI and AE on AP is 33.5%. Furthermore, the findings confirmed that EI significantly influences AE (β=0.316) with a variance (R2 ) of 10%, and AE significantly predicts AP (β=0.556). The implications of these findings suggest that enhancing students’ EI and AE can meaningfully improve their academic outcomes. The study contributes to the growing body of literature on EI, AE, and AP. Although the results may not be fully generalizable due to the pilot nature and limited sample size, they provide valuable insights and directions for future research in the context of Bangladeshi higher education.

Structure, Function, and Connectivity in The Multilingual Brain: A Systematic Review of Neurolinguistic Evidence and Implications for Language Education

The rapid development of neuroimaging technology has turned neurolinguistics into one of the most empirical areas of cognitive science, although the pedagogical translation into formal language education curricula remains disproportionately limited. This research addresses this gap by means of a mixed-methods study that triangulates three independent strands of data: a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) compliant Systematic Literature Review (SLR), a structured survey (N = 14) and a qualitative protocol comprising semi-structured interviews and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs).

English Language Assessment Policies and Pedagogical Realities in Bangladeshi Secondary Education

Assessment plays a critical role in educational systems, serving as a source of information on the achievement of learning goals and as a basis for important decisions on student evaluation, curriculum, and teaching methods. This study investigates the alignment between declared policies and actual practices of English language assessment in secondary schools of Dhaka, Bangladesh. The findings reveal a great discrepancy between the recommended assessment policies, which call for a comprehensive assessment of the four language skills (reading, writing, listening and speaking) and the common classroom practices where the assessment primarily centers around reading and writing, largely ignoring listening and speaking skills. This difference is further aggravated by such factors as large class size, insufficient training of teachers in communicative assessment techniques, and limited availability of multimedia resources. The paper concludes with strategic recommendations to bridge this policy-practice gap, including increased teacher professional development, integration of all four skills into formal assessment frameworks, and provision of essential technological infrastructure to foster a more holistic and effective English language learning environment in Bangladesh.

Attitudes Toward Animation-Based Instruction (ABI): A Multilevel Investigation of Adoption, Cognitive Engagement, Affective Orientations, Pedagogical Efficacy, and Academic Outcomes in Primary Classrooms

Although the theoretical basis for the use of animation-based instruction (ABI) is established in the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning and the Technology Acceptance Model, researchers’ attitudinal work has been limited to the use of unidimensional operationalisation, single-level analysis, and high resource contexts, which obscures the multilevel mechanisms that link teacher disposition to student outcomes. Drawing on CTML, TAM, the tripartite attitude model, and Self-Efficacy Theory, this study is the first to investigate the ABI adoption intention, cognitive engagement, affective orientations, pedagogical efficacy, and academic achievement in Bangladeshi primary EFL classrooms at multiple levels simultaneously. A sequential explanatory mixed-methods design was used: a cross-sectional multilevel survey with a quasi-experimental pretest–posttest component (N = 270 teachers; N ≈ 5,400 Grade III–V students; 90 government primary schools).

Financial Strain and Academic Performance: A Study at Public Universities in Bangladesh

Executive Summary:

Financial strain can greatly affect the academic performance of students in various ways, especially those students who are studying at the tertiary level of education. While they are at the crucial level of their study, concern regarding costs and expenditures with existing financial problems can harm them both physically and psychologically.

Statement of the problem – The study aimed to examine the barriers upheld by financial stress and propose potential strategies for mitigating these challenges.

Purpose statement – The study purposed to explore how financial strain affects the academic performance of the students of Bangladeshi public universities and ways to reduce and improve the situation with the help of existing facilities and support systems. This study will help in the field of tertiary-level education in Bangladesh as it will provide new knowledge about an area that has been under-researched in a specific context, geography, and population.

Limitations – The researchers found insufficient funding, time and manpower, lack of mobility, lack of enough previous literature, and lack of a large number of participants as some of the core limitations.

Methodology – The current study is exploratory research with the application of both qualitative and quantitative approaches. The participant count of the research is 149 students for the quantitative method and 2 students for the qualitative method participating from different public universities in Bangladesh. Structured questionnaires were provided to collect quantitative data, and interview questions were provided to collect qualitative data.

Result – The findings of this research indicate that financial strain significantly impedes the academic experience of the students, disrupts motivation and engagement in studies, and the available financial support system for the students is hardly effective.

Adoption of Semester System at a Higher Education Institution (HEI) in Bangladesh: Student Perceptions, Academic Pressures, and Scopes for Reform

Executive Summary:

Objectives: This study explores the adoption of the semester system in higher education institutions in Bangladesh, with particular attention to the University of Dhaka. Using a qualitative approach, the study examined students’ perceptions of the semester system in terms of its benefits, limitations, academic workload, learning outcomes, and contribution to employability.

Methods: Data were collected through interviews with 20 students from different departments, including both male and female participants, selected to capture varied academic experiences.

Results: The findings indicate that the semester system is appreciated for reducing session jams, encouraging continuous assessment, and developing job-related skills such as presentation, assignment writing, and viva voce performance. At the same time, participants reported several challenges, including insufficient time for study, excessive academic pressure, delayed result publication, limited teacher availability in some departments, and concerns about superficial learning under a shortened academic calendar.

Conclusions: The study suggests that while the semester system offers clear advantages in promoting timely academic progression and workplace-oriented learning, its effectiveness depends on proper implementation, adequate staffing, timely assessment, and a realistic semester duration. The study concludes with recommendations for extending the semester length, improving administrative efficiency, and strengthening instructional support to enhance learning quality in HEIs.

Implications: The findings carry direct implications for higher education policymakers, institutional administrators, and curriculum designers in developing countries. Realistic semester scheduling, equitable staffing, timely assessment, and sustained instructional support must be treated as non-negotiable policy priorities, not administrative variables, if semester-based reform is to deliver substantive, equitable learning outcomes at scale.

Exploring the Effectiveness of Formative Assessment in ESOL Students’ Learning at the Tertiary Level

Executive Summary:

This research explores the effectiveness of formative assessment in ESOL(English for Speakers of Other Languages) students learning at the tertiary level,University of Dhaka. It aims to identify common formative assessment strategies used by teachers, investigate student perceptions, and evaluate the role of formative assessment in academic improvement. The study employs a mixed-method approach involving surveys, interviews, and two focus group discussions (FGDs) with 50 students and 4 teachers. The research findings are expected to inform better teaching practices and enhance learner outcomes in tertiary ESOL classrooms. The results of the study indicate that teachers are more inclined to use questioning, both oral and written, as the major method for checking understanding. They also use different methods such as giving feedback, short tests, class tasks, group work, and presentations; however, these are only used occasionally and to a lesser extent. Pupils present a positive perspective towards formative assessment and they are of the opinion that regular feedback enables them to recognize their weaknesses, be motivated, and improve their general English skills. The findings also demonstrate that formative assessment plays a vital role in students' academic progress as it makes them more willing to participate and thus their confidence level is increased.

SDG 4 Alignment in the Primary-Level English Language Curriculum of Bangladesh: A Critical Qualitative Analysis

Executive Summary:

This study investigates the extent to which Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4) is reflected in the primary-level English curriculum of Bangladesh, encompassing Classes One through Five. The objective is to examine whether the English curriculum promotes quality learning outcomes, gender equity, inclusive education, global citizenship, and teacher professional development in consonance with the mandates of SDG 4. Employing qualitative document analysis, this study reviewed the National Curriculum Framework 2021 and the English for Today textbook series as authoritative national policy documents. The findings indicate that the English curriculum is systematically and progressively structured to develop language competencies across grade levels. It demonstrates gender-equitable visual and textual representations and integrates recurring themes of respect, cooperation, and environmental stewardship. Furthermore, embedded Teacher Notes serve as pedagogical scaffolds to support classroom practice. However, significant gaps persist: the representation of children with disabilities remains markedly insufficient, and effective curriculum implementation is frequently impeded by large class sizes and inadequate teacher training. It is concluded that, while the English curriculum exhibits considerable structural alignment with SDG 4, sustained teacher professional development and more robust inclusive practices are imperative to ensure that curricular intentions are translated into substantive learning outcomes for all learners.

The Impact of Qawmi Madrasa on Socio-Economic Mobility: A Study in the Context of Bangladeshi Madrasa Students and Teachers in Dhaka

Executive Summary:

Socioeconomic mobility involves the transition of individuals or groups from one social and economic status to another, which is determined by factors such as education, income, and available jobs. This paper presents a study on economic conditions, career aspirations, and educational barriers of Qawmi madrasa graduates. It also identifies the factors through which the above determinants influence the socioeconomic mobility of the Qawmi madrasa graduates and reveals the structural challenges that impede their integration into wider professional and academic spheres. Most of them make successful careers in religious and educational sectors; institutional barriers limit prospects for upward mobility. Policy interventions are needed to connect madrasa education with mainstream economic and educational systems. Improving degree recognition, language training, and skill development programs can make the socioeconomic conditions of madrasa graduates better so that they can more effectively contribute to Bangladesh’s changing job market. The present study aims to explore the impact of Qawmi Madrasa education on socioeconomic mobility in Bangladesh. The research has collected data through a mixed method. A total of 289 participants were selected for survey, 48 for interview, and 40 for Focus Group discussion (FGD). The results show a lack of socioeconomic mobility among Qawmi madrasa graduates because of limited educational opportunities, skills mismatch, and social barriers. Many of them remain involved in religious professions or self-employment, while others pursue business and vocational training for economic sustenance. The lack of proper government recognition, inadequate technical education, and language barriers further inhibit their entry into mainstream professions. Addressing these issues through curriculum reform, job placement programs, and policy support is essential for integrating madrasa graduates into the broader economy and expanding their career opportunities.

Determinants of Newsworthiness and Media Framing Across Journalistic Domains: A Qualitative Content Analysis of Political, Sports, and Policy News

Executive Summary:

Background and Purpose: The selection, construction, and dissemination of news are governed by a complex constellation of editorial values collectively described as newsworthiness. Despite a well-established theoretical tradition, empirical investigations into how these values manifest differentially across political, sporting, and policy-oriented journalistic domains remain comparatively limited. This study addresses that gap by systematically examining the newsworthiness and framing strategies evident in three purposively selected news articles drawn from internationally and nationally recognised media outlets.

Design/Methodology/Approach: Adopting a qualitative content analysis methodology informed by Galtung and Ruge's (1965) foundational news value taxonomy, Harcup and O'Neill's (2001) revised framework, and Entman's (1993) framing theory, this study analyses three articles: "Sheikh Hasina's Final Hours as a Hated Autocrat" (BBC News), "Tamim and Co Triumph as 'Power and Experience' Combine" (The Daily Star), and "Exams for Public Jobs: Govt to Raise Age Limit to 32 Years" (The Daily Star). Each article is evaluated against five canonical newsworthiness criteria , timeliness, impact, prominence, conflict, and human interest , as well as through a systematic assessment of media framing and adherence to journalistic ethics.

Findings: The analysis reveals that while all three articles satisfy the criterion of timeliness, they diverge substantially along the axes of impact, conflict intensity, and human interest. The political article exhibited the highest degree of multi-criteria newsworthiness, incorporating all five values at elevated levels, while the sports and policy articles demonstrated context-specific configurations of these criteria. Framing strategies varied markedly, from the conflict-centred, evaluatively charged language deployed in the BBC article to the affirmative, solidarity-invoking framing of the sports report and the informationally restrained register of the policy article. Significant ethical tensions, particularly regarding sensationalism and subjective characterisation in political reporting, were identified.

Originality/Value: This study contributes to the scholarly understanding of journalistic gatekeeping and media framing by providing a cross-domain comparative analysis grounded in both classical and contemporary news value theory. The findings carry implications for journalism education, media literacy curricula, and the professional training of journalists in navigating the intersection of newsworthiness, audience engagement, and ethical responsibility.

Linguistic Identity Crisis and Fragmentation among Students in English-Medium Schools in Urban Bangladesh

Executive Summary:

Background & Rationale. The global proliferation of English-medium instruction (EMI) in postcolonial contexts has generated a contested terrain where institutional language policies intersect with the lived sociolinguistic realities of students, producing consequences that extend well beyond academic performance into the very formation of self. In urban Bangladesh, a nation constituted through a violent linguistic nationalism whose foundational act of independence was itself precipitated by the struggle to defend Bangla in 1952, English-medium schooling represents a deeply paradoxical institution: one that simultaneously confers socioeconomic capital and quietly erodes the mother-tongue foundations upon which cultural identity is built. Despite a growing body of scholarship on language ideology, multilingualism, and identity in South Asian education, the specific psycholinguistic and sociocultural dynamics of linguistic identity crisis and fragmentation among students enrolled in English-medium schools in urban Bangladesh remain remarkably undertheorized, empirically underdeveloped, and largely absent from international academic discourse.

Purpose. This study examines how students in English-medium schools in urban Bangladesh experience, negotiate, and articulate processes of linguistic identity crisis and fragmentation, understood as the ontological rupture and disintegration of a coherent sense of self arising from competing, often irreconcilable, linguistic demands across institutional, familial, and social domains.

Methodology. Adopting a qualitative research design rooted in interpretive phenomenology, this study employs in-depth semi-structured interviews, focus group discussions, and reflective narrative inquiry with purposively selected participants, including secondary-level students, their parents, and teachers, drawn from English-medium schools across Dhaka city. Data were analyzed using thematic analysis informed by reflexive and constructivist epistemological commitments, with iterative coding procedures ensuring analytical rigor, credibility, and theoretical saturation.

Findings. The analysis reveals three interrelated dimensions of linguistic identity crisis among participants: (i) intergenerational linguistic estrangement, wherein students report emotional and communicative distance from parents and grandparents attributable to asymmetric language proficiencies; (ii) code-switching as a survival performance, in which students develop fluid but fragile hybrid linguistic repertoires that simultaneously signal aspiration and camouflage a deep-seated sense of linguistic inauthenticity; and (iii) institutional linguicism and the internalization of shame, manifesting as internalized hierarchies in which Bangla is tacitly coded as inferior, producing affective states of embarrassment, ambivalence, and in some cases outright hostility toward the mother tongue. Collectively, these findings construct a phenomenological portrait of students who are linguistically in-between, neither fully English-proficient enough to belong to the global imaginary the medium promises, nor sufficiently Bangla-confident to remain rooted in their cultural and national inheritance.

Contribution. This study makes an original theoretical contribution by advancing the construct of diglossic self-fragmentation as an analytically productive lens for understanding linguistic identity disruption in postcolonial EMI contexts, extending beyond existing binaries of additive versus subtractive bilingualism. It further contributes empirically rich, voice-centered evidence from a geopolitically and culturally specific context that has received disproportionately little attention in international education research. The findings carry significant implications for language-in-education policy, curriculum design, school leadership, and teacher professional development in Bangladesh and analogous Global South contexts navigating the tensions between colonial linguistic legacies and the demands of contemporary globalization.

Beyond MOOCs: A Secure Institutional Digital Learning Ecosystem for Interdisciplinary Education and Entrepreneurial Innovation

Executive Summary:

Interdisciplinary education has emerged as a critical enabler of innovation and entrepreneurial development in the twenty-first century. Students frequently seek knowledge that extends beyond their primary fields of study,a Life Sciences student may wish to explore Artificial Intelligence or Data Analytics, while a student in Information and Communication Technology (ICT) may derive significant benefit from engagement with biotechnology or healthcare systems. Yet universities commonly provide insufficient institutional support for such cross-disciplinary exploration. Concurrently, the collaboration between universities and industry remains structurally limited, with interactions typically confined to internship placements or recruitment-related activities rather than encompassing continuous knowledge exchange, sustained mentorship, collaborative research, and innovation-driven learning. Strengthening these inter-sectoral relationships is essential for preparing graduates who are capable of transforming academic knowledge into practical, entrepreneurial solutions.

To address these systemic challenges, this paper proposes an Integrated Digital Learning Ecosystem (IDLE) that substantially extends the conventional capabilities of MOOCs. The proposed framework establishes a secure institutional platform through which authenticated university members can explore cross-departmental learning opportunities while preserving academic governance and institutional security via official university credentials. Beyond enabling interdisciplinary curriculum exploration, the platform facilitates structured industry–academia collaboration through expert-led seminars, mentorship programmes, collaborative projects, innovation competitions, and entrepreneurship-oriented initiatives. Personalized learning recommendations and digital micro-credentials further support continuous skill development and career readiness.

The primary contribution of this study is the conceptual design of a secure, university-centred digital learning ecosystem that integrates interdisciplinary education, institutional learning management, industry engagement, and entrepreneurial innovation within a unified framework. By bridging departmental boundaries and strengthening the collaborative networks among students, faculty members, and industry professionals, the proposed model aspires to cultivate multidisciplinary competencies, promote a culture of innovation, and more effectively prepare graduates for the demands of the digital economy.
